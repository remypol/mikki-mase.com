## How to use these tools

Run the numbers before the session, not during it. The point of every tool here is the same: know your edge (or
know you do not have one), size so variance cannot bust you, and turn every loss into a smaller loss through the
rebate. Fill in your own numbers in the blanks.

---

## The Bet-Sizing Tool (a practical Kelly)

Kelly tells you the fraction of your bankroll to bet so you grow as fast as possible without risking ruin. The
honest version most players need:

- **If you do not have an edge, your Kelly bet is zero.** Bet the minimum or do not bet. No system makes a
  negative-edge bet a good bet.
- **When you do have an edge,** bet a small fraction of your bankroll proportional to that edge, and bet LESS than
  full Kelly (half-Kelly is the common pro choice) because over-betting sharply raises your risk of ruin.
- More bankroll units behind you = lower risk of a normal losing streak busting you.

**Worked example:** bankroll $10,000, a modest edge, half-Kelly suggests a unit around 1 to 2 percent of bankroll,
so roughly $100 to $200 a hand, not $1,000. The discipline is in the size, not the guts.

`Your bankroll: $__________   Your unit (1-2%): $__________   Hard cap per hand: $__________`

> PULLQUOTE: Over-betting is how players with a real edge still go broke. Size small, survive the swings.

## The Loss-Rebate Calculator

A loss rebate gives you back a percentage of your net losses. It is the single tool that can flip the math, and
most players never ask for one.

**The math:** if your expected loss is L and the rebate is R percent, your effective expected loss is
`L x (1 - R)`. A high enough rebate can take a slightly-negative game toward break-even.

**Rebate ranges:** roughly 5 to 7 percent for a new player, up to ~25 percent for an established high-volume
player. Always get it **in writing**, and start at a smaller property where your action means more to them, then
use that as leverage at the bigger ones.

**Worked example:** an expected net loss of about $25,000 over a run, with a ~20 percent rebate negotiated across
properties, becomes a net of roughly $14,100. The rebate clawed back about $11,000 of expected losses, without
changing how you played.

`Your expected loss: $__________   x (1 - your rebate __________%) = effective loss $__________`

> PULLQUOTE: They think the rebate buys your loyalty. You are using their money to erase their edge.

## The Pre-Session Checklist

Run this before every session. If any answer is no, you do not sit.

1. Is this the right game and the right table (3:2 blackjack, no CSM, Banker on baccarat, face-up Pai Gow)?
2. Is my session bankroll separate from money I need for life?
3. Have I written my win limit and my loss limit, and will I actually leave on either?
4. Is my unit size 1 to 2 percent of bankroll, with a hard cap per hand?
5. Do I have a rebate or comp arrangement in place, in writing?
6. Have I set my 30 to 45 minute clock?
7. Am I clear-headed (not tired, not tilted, not drinking)?

- Taxes lesson: multiple legal/compliance errors and high‑risk oversimplifications
  - W‑2G thresholds: “table game wins ≥ $5,000 at ≥300:1 odds” is wrong. The filing threshold for “other wagering” (which can include some table propositions) is proceeds of $600 or more and at least 300× the amount wagered. The $5,000 threshold applies to net poker tournament winnings (and to trigger mandatory 24% withholding for certain 300× wins), not to W‑2G issuance generally.
  - Professional gambler/self‑employment tax: Stating flatly “you pay self‑employment tax on net winnings” is not safe. There is longstanding controversy and IRS/Tax Court guidance cutting both ways. As of our knowledge cutoff (and pre‑2026), IRS CCA memoranda and cases have indicated that gambling winnings may not constitute self‑employment income for SECA, even for pros. Do not assert SE tax applies as a blanket rule—flag as factspecific and CPA‑only.
  - Pro gambler deductions: The effect of IRC §165(d) changes under the TCJA (2018–2025) limited a professional gambler’s “business expenses” to the extent of gambling winnings. Whether that limitation continues post‑2025 is time‑sensitive and uncertain as of our 2024 knowledge cutoff. Your current language (“losses offset winnings directly on Schedule C; you can deduct travel, lodging…”) is too categorical and could be wrong depending on year. Replace with a dated, conditional statement and a CPA directive.
  - State list: The list of states that disallow loss deductions (Illinois, Indiana, Massachusetts, Michigan, Ohio, West Virginia, Wisconsin, others) is risky and likely inaccurate for some of those states and for residents vs nonresidents. Remove the specific list and replace with “many states differ; some allow loss offsets, others do not—check your state’s current rules.”
  - “Comp value is technically taxable income”: This is not settled, and Publication 525’s “winnings include the FMV of prizes” does not clearly cover standard casino comps, which many practitioners treat as price rebates (not taxable). This “technically taxable” assertion is overconfident and could mislead. Soften to “often treated as non‑taxable promotional rebates; treatment can vary—ask your CPA.”
  - “Wins credited to your front money account are treated as received the moment they hit the account”: Constructive receipt is nuanced. This is too definitive; soften to “often treated as constructively received when available to you—ask your CPA about timing.”
